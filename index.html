About

Built for the person at the end of a long shift.

I trained as a nurse at Chung Shan Medical University in Taiwan, then worked with older adults and families at Belmont Village and with kids at a pediatric dental office here in the Bay Area.

My rule is simple. If a tired person can't understand it at a glance, it isn't done yet.

Portfolio 3 · 3D printing · 2017

3D Modeling Software Study

A high-school research project that compared 3D modeling tools, picked the one a beginner could actually learn, and took a design from the browser to a printed part.

Context

Second-year electronics project at Taipei Municipal Daan Vocational High School, 2017. Team of two, with a faculty advisor.

The problem

3D printing was already used in education, art, and construction, and was moving into communications, aerospace, and automotive work. We wanted to learn additive manufacturing by making something ourselves, and as beginners we first had to choose a tool we could learn within a project course.

Key decisions

  1. Compared three tools before choosing. Blender: as capable as 3ds Max or Maya, suited to people who plan to go deep. SketchUp: clean interface and a shared model library, suited to architects and designers. Tinkercad: one of the simplest, free, and it runs in the browser.
  2. Chose Tinkercad for how it is used, not for its feature list. The interface is simple and quick to pick up, and the web version removes the download and install step.
  3. Split the work into two tracks. First learn the modeling software, then learn the printer, then bring the two together in one printed piece.
  4. Learned the printer part by part. For the school's H360 3D printer we documented the platform, the nozzle, and the frame, then the operating steps: loading filament, reading a file from the SD card, preheating, and positioning the nozzle.
  5. Wrote down every step with a screenshot, so the process can be repeated: place a basic shape, adjust its dimensions, then use a "hole" shape to cut openings.

Portfolio 4 · Electronics build

Electrical Music Box

An electronic music box I built by hand: a circuit board, a transformer, and a speaker wired into a metal case.

What it is

The board drives a small speaker and is powered from the wall through a 15 V transformer. A power LED sits on the board, and the controls come out through the side panel.

My part

I soldered the board, then assembled and wired the case.

What the build involved

  1. Two kinds of soldering on one board. Through-hole resistors, diodes, transistors, and capacitors, plus small surface-mount parts.
  2. Wall power kept apart from the board. The power cord and transformer sit on one side of the case, with the cables tied down.
  3. Everything fixed to the case. The board is raised on standoffs and the speaker is bolted to the panel, so nothing moves when the box is carried.
  4. Built to be checked. The board has test points and trimmers for measuring and adjusting the circuit after assembly.

I also hold an electronics certification.
